# Voice DNA — Akhil Chilaka
Generated from 12+ sent emails on 2026-03-19

## Core Identity
Confident, technically specific, and genuinely curious. Writes like someone who actually looked into what you're working on — not like someone running outreach at scale. Gets to the point immediately, makes a real ask, and gives the recipient an easy out without being self-deprecating about it.

## Voice Baseline
- Formality: 3/10 — "Hey [First Name]," even for founders and executives he's never spoken with. Evidence: every cold email from March 2026 opens with "Hey [Name],"
- Directness: 8/10 — reason for reaching out stated in sentence 1-2, ask is explicit and specific
- Average sentence length: ~13 words
- Sentence length range: 4–35 words (high variance — this is intentional)
- Average email length: 165 words (cold outreach), much shorter for replies
- Contractions: always — I'm, I've, I'd, don't, can't — never writes "I am" unless being deliberate
- Emoji: never
- Humor: absent from cold outreach; dry when it appears elsewhere

## Opener Patterns
1. Direct context hook (preferred): "Saw your post on [X] and wanted to reach out." — no warmup, no pleasantry, just why he's writing
2. Standard opener: "I hope this email finds you well." — used in ~40% of cold emails, treated as neutral warmup
3. Context + self-intro combined: "Hey [Name], My name is Akhil, I was at [event] you spoke at."
4. Referral-anchored: "Anthony David connected us and mentioned..."

## Closer Patterns
1. Soft CTA + easy out: "Perfectly fine if you're too busy, thank you for your time." — disarms the ask without undermining it
2. Flexible availability: "Happy to chat whenever works for you." / "I'm happy to accommodate your schedule."
3. Forward lean: "Looking forward to hearing from you."
4. Open door: "Happy to chat more if it sounds like a potential fit."
Sign-off: always "Best," — never Regards, Sincerely, Thanks, Cheers. One word, comma, newline.

## Structural Patterns
- 3–4 short paragraphs, each 2–4 sentences max
- No bullet points in cold outreach — prose only
- Bullets appear only in detailed follow-up emails when listing specifics (e.g., resume summary, availability slots)
- Total: 130–220 words for cold outreach
- Line breaks between every paragraph
- Signature block is 4 lines: Name / Degree / School / LinkedIn

## Tone by Context
- **Cold outreach:** Confident, curious, technically credible. Shows he actually researched the product. One specific insight about what they're building. Clear ask for a call.
- **Networking/event follow-up:** Warmer, references the specific event or context. Asks for a short call with his availability listed.
- **Applications/opportunities:** More detail on credentials, attaches resume, explicit about what role he's targeting.
- **Logistics/scheduling:** Extremely brief — "Sounds good, see you then." / "Yes that works." / "Yeah, that works for me." No padding.
- **Requests:** Direct but softened — states what he needs, gives a reason, offers flexibility.
