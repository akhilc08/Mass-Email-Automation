# Akhil Chilaka — Email Voice & Tone Profile

*Derived from analysis of 12+ cold outreach emails written Feb–Mar 2026.*

---

## Greeting

Almost always uses **"Hey [First Name],"** — even for senior executives and founders at companies he's never spoken with. Occasionally uses **"Dear Mr./Ms. [Last Name],"** in very formal or academic contexts, but this is increasingly rare. The default is casual and first-name.

---

## Opening Line

Two patterns, depending on how warm the context is:

- **Direct/context-anchored** (preferred): References exactly how he found them or what caught his attention. Example: *"Saw your post on the Cornell entrepreneurship list and wanted to reach out."* No filler, no pleasantries — just the reason he's there.
- **Standard opener** (colder outreach): *"I hope this email finds you well."* Used when there's no specific hook to reference.

---

## Self-Introduction

Always introduces himself early — usually sentence 1 or 2 of the first paragraph.

- Preferred form: **"My name is Saiakhil Chilaka and I'm a CS student at Cornell."** or **"I'm Akhil, a CS student at Cornell."**
- Always includes Cornell University and CS (often "CS & Math")
- Uses "Akhil" informally, "Saiakhil Chilaka" in the signature

---

## Credibility

Shows credentials through 1–2 **specific, relevant accomplishments** — never a generic skill list. Connects skills directly to what the recipient is working on.

- Names specific tech he's used: React, TypeScript, Python, Next.js, PostgreSQL, PyTorch, Claude SDK, GPT-4
- References real projects: Coast (wholesale vehicle marketplace), skin cancer detection app (featured on CNN, recognized by NC governor), Cornell Data Strategy, VHB internship
- Example: *"I've built real-time dashboards with Next.js and Supabase handling live data at scale, and developed AI-powered tools using the Claude SDK to parse and process unstructured inputs."*
- The accomplishments are always chosen to directly connect to the recipient's domain — never copy-pasted across emails

---

## Personalization

Shows he actually researched what they're building. Articulates *why* it interests him, not just that it does.

- Frames interest as: *"The trust layer problem for agentic commerce is one of those things that feels obvious in retrospect but nobody's really nailed yet."*
- Or: *"the way Bujo is building conversational AI agents that actually resolve customer requests end to end, rather than just deflecting them, is really fascinating to me."*
- Specific product insight > generic enthusiasm every time

---

## The Ask

Clear, specific, and low-pressure:

- *"a brief call"* / *"a quick 20-minute call"*
- Often includes specific availability: *"Thursday: after 3pm / Friday: after 1pm / Sunday: anytime"*
- Frequently softens with an easy out: *"Perfectly fine if you're too busy, thank you for your time."* or *"Happy to chat whenever works for you."*
- Frames himself as flexible: *"I'm happy to accommodate your schedule."*

---

## Closing

Always the same:

- **Sign-off**: *"Best,"* — no exceptions (never "Regards," "Sincerely," "Thanks,")
- **Name in signature**: *Saiakhil Chilaka*
- **Signature lines**: CS & Math | Cornell University | LinkedIn: linkedin.com/in/akhilchilaka

---

## Length & Structure

- **3–4 short paragraphs**, each 2–4 sentences
- **~150–250 words** total for cold outreach
- **No bullet points** in cold outreach (bullets appear only in detailed follow-up emails)
- No filler sentences — every sentence earns its place

---

## Recurring Phrases

- *"I'd love to..."* / *"I would love to..."*
- *"Happy to..."*
- *"really fascinating to me"*
- *"caught my attention"* / *"caught my attention immediately"*
- *"A bit about me:"* / *"A quick intro:"*
- *"Looking forward to hearing from you"*
- *"Happy to chat more if it sounds like a potential fit"*

---

## What He Avoids

- *"I am writing to express my interest in..."* — never
- Excessive compliments or flattery before making his point
- Vague credential dumps ("I have experience in many areas...")
- Long paragraphs or walls of text
- Corporate buzzwords and jargon
- Desperation or over-qualification
- Restating the recipient's company description back to them

---

## Overall Tone

**Confident, curious, and direct.** He writes like someone who genuinely finds the work interesting — not like someone running through a checklist. He's humble without being self-deprecating, and assertive about the ask without being pushy. He gives the recipient an easy out and means it.
